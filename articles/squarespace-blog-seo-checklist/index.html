Squarespace blog SEO checklist before you publish

A practical check of your article, SEO settings, links, images, and live page before you call it finished.

Before you publish a Squarespace blog post, check that it answers a clear question, has an accurate title and description, uses a readable URL, and works on a phone. Then confirm that the live page is accessible and inspect it in Google Search Console.

This checklist is for a small business owner publishing a useful article on Squarespace 7.1. Keep the post unpublished while you work through it. The final section covers what to check after it goes live.

Decide what the reader should leave knowing

Write one sentence before you edit anything else: “After reading this, the reader will be able to ___.”

For example, a photographer could write: “After reading this, a client will know what to wear to an outdoor family photo session.” That is a manageable article. “Everything about family photography” is a much harder promise to deliver.

Read the opening paragraph against that sentence. Does it begin answering the question, or spend several paragraphs announcing that the topic is important? Replace the warm-up with the advice the reader came for.

Then add something specific: an example, a comparison, a useful decision rule, or an explanation drawn from your work. For the photographer, that could be a sample outfit combination and the reason it works. Use actual experience where you have it, and label invented examples as examples.

Check the headline and SEO title separately

The headline introduces the article on the page. The SEO title is a separate setting, and the URL is separate again. Squarespace explains these distinctions in its page-title guide.

Use a title that names the subject and makes a promise the article keeps. “What to Wear for Outdoor Family Photos” tells a prospective client more than “Our Latest Photography Tips.” Avoid repeating slight variations of the same keyword.

Google can choose title text from several page signals, so your preferred SEO title is not a guaranteed search-result headline. Its guidance favors descriptive, concise titles. Google title guidance

In Squarespace 7.1, open Pages, select your blog, hover over the post, choose the three-dot menu, then Settings. The SEO tab contains the SEO title and description. The Content tab contains the Post URL, excerpt, author and featured image. Blog posts use the classic editor. Squarespace post settings

Write a description that previews the answer

Describe what someone will learn from this particular post. “Practical advice on layers, colors and shoes for an outdoor family photo session” is more useful than a general description of the photographer’s business.

The SEO description is different from the excerpt that introduces a post in your blog listing. Give each a clear purpose. Squarespace SEO descriptions

Keep the description readable and faithful to the article. Google often creates snippets from the page itself and may use your description when it fits the search. There is no universal 160-character rule that guarantees the full snippet will appear. Google snippet guidance

Review the complete URL before sharing it

Check the Post URL field before publication. A slug such as “outdoor-family-photo-outfits” is easier to recognize than an accidental string of numbers.

Inspect the complete address as well. A Squarespace blog URL includes the collection’s URL and the post portion; its configured format may also include a date. Choose the intended address before distributing links. If you later change an already published URL, account for the old address with a redirect. Squarespace URL slugs

For a new post, record the final address in your publishing notes. That gives you one exact URL to check, share and measure.

Review every claim an AI draft added

If Claude or another assistant helped write the post, look for sentences that sound authoritative without evidence. Check prices, product features, quotations, instructions and statistics against the original source. Delete invented anecdotes and unsupported claims of personal experience.

A practical review method is to highlight every sentence that contains a number, a named product capability, or an assertion such as “always” or “guaranteed.” Verify it, narrow it, or remove it. Check that each linked source actually supports the adjacent statement.

Google’s guidance allows AI to help with research and structure, while emphasizing accuracy and human review. Producing many pages without adding value can violate its scaled-content-abuse policy. Apply the review to titles, descriptions and image text as well as the article body. Google guidance on AI content

Add links that help the reader continue

Link to a relevant next step where it naturally belongs. In the photographer’s example, that might be a page explaining how family sessions work. Use link text that describes the destination.

Also add a link to the new article from a relevant existing page. Google recommends contextual internal links and says important pages should be linked from another page on the site. There is no universal ideal link count. Google link guidance

Click every link yourself. Confirm that it reaches the intended page and that any booking, contact or download link completes the action promised beside it.

Check images and read the post on a phone

Open each meaningful image and review its alternative text. In a Squarespace image block, use the pencil icon, then Content → Image alt text. Describe the relevant visual information instead of filling the field with search phrases. Squarespace’s SEO Report does not scan images in collection items, including blog posts, so review those images directly. Squarespace alt-text guide

Read the whole draft on a phone. Check whether screenshots are legible, captions explain what matters, headings make the article easy to scan, and buttons are comfortable to tap. A screenshot that looks clear on a laptop may need a tighter crop or a written explanation on a smaller display.

Choose a featured image that belongs with the article. Check the excerpt and sharing image too, so the article is recognizable when someone encounters it outside the reading page.

Confirm access and search settings

Keep unfinished work private. When the article is ready for launch, confirm that the intended public page opens without an editor login or password, and review the site's crawler controls and blog collection’s search-visibility settings.

Squarespace does not give individual blog posts the same “Hide page from search results” switch used by ordinary pages. The collection settings matter: “All pages in this collection” can hide both the blog landing page and its posts. Review the scope before changing anything. Squarespace search visibility and blog collection controls

Being publicly accessible and being permitted to appear in search are separate checks. Confirm both for the page you intend readers to find.

Inspect the published page

After publishing, open the final URL in a private browser window and repeat the reading and link checks. Verify the actual page rather than relying only on its editor preview.

Use Google Search Console’s URL Inspection tool for that address. The indexed result describes what Google currently knows; Test live URL checks the page as it can be fetched now. A successful live test does not prove that the page is indexed. You can request indexing, but Google does not guarantee inclusion or a completion time. Google URL Inspection guidance

Record the publication date, final URL and any issue the inspection identifies. Review performance as data accumulates, and keep a note of substantive revisions so you can interpret later changes.

Copy this publishing checklist

  • The opening answers a specific reader question.
  • The article contains a useful example or original explanation.
  • The headline, SEO title and description accurately describe the content.
  • The complete URL is intentional.
  • Facts, sources and AI-added claims have been reviewed.
  • Links work, and a relevant existing page links to this article.
  • Images have been reviewed directly, including their alternative text.
  • The post reads well on a phone.
  • Public access and the relevant search settings are correct for launch.
  • The published URL has been checked and recorded.

Work through the checks in order. Complete the final URL inspection after publishing. Once the page is live, keep using the checklist whenever you make a substantial update.
